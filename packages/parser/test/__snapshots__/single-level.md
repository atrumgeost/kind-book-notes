# Grow Your Garden!

## A New Kind of Soil

“Gardening is not a gift. It is a habit of attention.”—Rosa Almeida

---

You don’t really find your harvest; it finds you.

---

Imagine if your neighbor already knew your tomatoes because she walks past your fence every morning.

## 1. You Don’t Have to Be an Expert.

There are many myths about green thumbs, and the most stubborn one is the “born gardener” myth: someone who appears with perfect instincts and never kills a single fern.

---

“a whole street of growers

---

In a shared plot, everyone—the beginner and the veteran, the planner and the improviser—has something to offer.

---

Plant what you love, and the people who love the same plants will find you.

## 2. Think Roots, Not Flowers.

*🖼 Image highlight, not included in the Kindle export (Page 22 · Location 149).*

---

When a gardener talks about her “garden,” she could mean two things: the finished bed in July, or the daily work of turning soil, pulling weeds, and waiting.

---

Keep a garden journal. Photograph the same corner every week. Write down what failed.

## 3. Share One Seed Every Day.

Labels matter. A pot that says “grandmother’s basil” gets watered more carefully than one that says nothing at all.

> [!note]
> makes me think about labeling the balcony herbs

---

Every seed packet tells a small story: where it came from, how it grows, what it becomes.
